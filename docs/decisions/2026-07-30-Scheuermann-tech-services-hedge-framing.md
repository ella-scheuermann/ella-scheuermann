# FX Receivable Hedge Framing Memo

**To:** Chief Financial Officer  
**From:** Ella Scheuermann  
**Date:** 2026-07-30  
**Re:** Managing Foreign Exchange Risk for a €12.5 Million Receivable

## Executive Summary

Our firm expects to receive €12,500,000 from a European customer in one year. Although the payment amount is fixed in euros, its value in U.S. dollars will depend on the EUR/USD exchange rate when the payment is received. At the current exchange rate of 1.0910 USD/EUR, the receivable is worth approximately $13.64 million. If the euro weakens before settlement, the company would receive fewer U.S. dollars than expected, creating uncertainty in cash flow and financial planning. To manage this risk, the company should evaluate three common hedging strategies: forward contracts, money market hedges, and currency options. The remaining stages of this project will compare these alternatives using current market data to determine which strategy provides the best balance of risk reduction, flexibility, and cost.

## Background

As a U.S. technology services firm, the company is expecting a €12,500,000 payment from a European customer in one year. While the amount of euros to be received is fixed, the value of those euros in U.S. dollars can change significantly before settlement. If the euro depreciates, the company will receive fewer dollars than anticipated, reducing revenue and making budgeting more difficult. Because exchange rates can fluctuate considerably over a year, managing this foreign exchange exposure is an important part of reducing financial risk.

## Method

This analysis will compare the three primary methods used to hedge foreign exchange risk. A forward contract will be evaluated as a way to lock in an exchange rate and eliminate uncertainty. A money market hedge will be examined to determine how borrowing and investing can create a similar fixed exchange rate. Finally, a currency option will be considered because it protects against unfavorable exchange rate movements while still allowing the company to benefit if the euro strengthens. Market rates and pricing data will be incorporated during the later stages of the project.

## Findings

Each hedging strategy offers different advantages and trade-offs. A forward contract provides certainty by locking in today's exchange rate, but it also eliminates the opportunity to benefit if the euro appreciates. A money market hedge produces a similar level of certainty, although it requires additional financing and more complex transactions. A currency option protects the company from unfavorable exchange rate movements while preserving the potential to benefit from favorable ones, but this flexibility comes at the cost of an upfront premium. The best choice will depend on the company's priorities, including its tolerance for risk and willingness to pay for additional flexibility.

## Implications

With a receivable of this size, leaving the exposure unhedged could have a meaningful impact on the company's U.S. dollar cash flows if exchange rates move unfavorably. Carefully evaluating the available hedging strategies will help reduce uncertainty, improve budgeting accuracy, and support more informed financial decision-making.

## Limitations & Next Steps

This memo serves as an initial assessment of the company's foreign exchange exposure and does not recommend a specific hedging strategy. In Phase 2, the project will develop the technical specifications for each hedge. Phase 3 will focus on building and auditing the valuation model with AI assistance. Phase 4 will incorporate current market exchange rates and interest rates into the analysis. Finally, Phase 5 will compare the results of each strategy and recommend the most effective hedge based on quantitative analysis.
