# FX Receivable Hedge Framing Memo - U.S. Tech Services Firm

**To:** Chief Financial Officer  
**From:** Ella Scheuermann  
**Date:** 2026-07-30  
**Re:** Managing Foreign Exchange Risk for a €12.5 Million Receivable

## Executive Summary

Our firm expects to receive €12,500,000 from a European customer in one year. Although the payment amount is fixed in euros, its value in U.S. dollars will depend on the EUR/USD exchange rate when the payment is received. At the current exchange rate of 1.0910 USD/EUR, the receivable is worth approximately $13.64 million. If the euro weakens before settlement, the company would receive fewer U.S. dollars than expected, creating uncertainty in cash flow and financial planning. For example, if the euro depreciated by 10% before settlement, the value of the receivable would decrease by approximately $1.36 million. To manage this risk, the company should evaluate three common hedging strategies: forward contracts, money market hedges, and currency options. The remaining stages of this project will compare these alternatives using current market data to determine which strategy provides the best balance of risk reduction, flexibility, and cost.

## Background & Objectives

As a U.S. technology services firm, the company is expecting a €12,500,000 payment from a European customer in one year. While the amount of euros to be received is fixed, the value of those euros in U.S. dollars can change significantly before settlement. If the euro depreciates, the company will receive fewer dollars than anticipated, reducing revenue and making budgeting more difficult. Because exchange rates can fluctuate considerably over a year, managing this foreign exchange exposure is an important part of reducing financial risk. The objective of this project is to evaluate the available hedging strategies and identify the approach that best reduces foreign exchange risk while balancing cost and flexibility.

## Methods

This analysis will compare the three primary methods used to hedge foreign exchange risk. A forward contract will be evaluated as a way to lock in an exchange rate and eliminate uncertainty. A money market hedge will be examined to determine how borrowing and investing can create a similar fixed exchange rate. Finally, a currency option will be evaluated because it provides protection if the euro weakens while allowing the company to benefit if the euro strengthens. Market rates and pricing data will be incorporated during the later stages of the project.


## Limitations & Next Steps

This memo serves as an initial assessment of the company's foreign exchange exposure and does not recommend a specific hedging strategy. In Phase 2, the project will develop the technical specifications for each hedge. Phase 3 will focus on building and auditing the valuation model with AI assistance. Phase 4 will incorporate current market exchange rates and interest rates into the analysis. Finally, Phase 5 will compare the results of each strategy and recommend the most effective hedge based on quantitative analysis.
