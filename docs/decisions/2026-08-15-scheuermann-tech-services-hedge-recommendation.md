# FX Hedging Recommendation Memorandum

**To:** Chief Financial Officer  
**From:** Ella Scheuermann  
**Date:** August 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Foreign Exchange Hedge Recommendation for EUR 12,500,000 Receivable

---

# A. Exposure Summary

The company expects to receive **EUR 12,500,000** in one year from a European customer. Because the receivable is denominated in euros while the company's financial reporting and operating currency is U.S. dollars, the firm is exposed to foreign exchange risk. If the euro depreciates before settlement, the value of the receivable in U.S. dollars will decline. The objective of the hedge analysis is to evaluate several available hedging strategies and recommend the approach that best protects the company's cash flows while considering cost, flexibility, and potential upside.

---

# B. Hedge Outcomes

Five alternatives were evaluated using current market data: remaining unhedged, entering into a forward contract, implementing a money-market hedge, purchasing a put option, and evaluating a call option for comparison.

The **unhedged position** provides the greatest potential gain if the euro appreciates, but it also exposes the company to unlimited downside if the euro weakens. This strategy offers no protection against adverse exchange-rate movements and produces the highest earnings volatility.

The **forward hedge** locks in a known exchange rate today, guaranteeing a fixed U.S. dollar cash inflow at settlement regardless of future exchange-rate movements. This strategy eliminates exchange-rate uncertainty and provides the highest level of budget certainty.

The **money-market hedge** produces a nearly identical outcome to the forward hedge because both strategies are based on covered interest parity. The money-market hedge requires borrowing, converting currencies immediately, and investing the proceeds until maturity. While financially effective, it is operationally more complex than using a forward contract.

The **put option hedge** establishes a minimum exchange rate while allowing the company to benefit if the euro appreciates above the strike price. The option premium represents the cost of maintaining this flexibility. Although the put hedge generally produces lower proceeds than the forward when exchange rates remain stable, it preserves upside potential that the forward contract sacrifices.

The **call option** was included for comparison purposes only. Because the company expects to receive euros rather than purchase them, a call option is not an appropriate primary hedge for this exposure.

---

# C. Sensitivity Interpretation

The sensitivity analysis illustrates how each strategy responds to changes in the ending EUR/USD exchange rate.

If the euro depreciates below the current spot rate, the forward and money-market hedges provide stable cash flows because the exchange rate is effectively locked in before settlement. The put option also limits downside risk by establishing a minimum exchange rate, although the premium reduces the net proceeds compared to the forward hedge.

If the euro appreciates, the unhedged position produces the highest proceeds because the company fully benefits from the stronger euro. The put option also participates in this appreciation after accounting for the premium, while the forward and money-market hedges remain fixed regardless of market movements.

These results highlight the trade-off between certainty and flexibility. The forward and money-market hedges maximize predictability, whereas the put option provides insurance while preserving potential upside.

---

# D. Recommendation

Based on the live market data and completed hedge analysis, I recommend that the company use a **forward hedge** to manage its foreign exchange exposure.

The company's primary objective should be protecting the expected U.S. dollar value of a significant foreign-currency receivable. The forward contract provides complete protection against adverse exchange-rate movements while delivering a known cash inflow that simplifies budgeting, financial planning, and cash-flow forecasting.

Although the put option preserves upside potential if the euro appreciates, this flexibility comes at the cost of the option premium. Unless management has a strong expectation that the euro will strengthen materially over the next year, paying the premium is unlikely to provide sufficient additional value compared to the certainty offered by the forward contract.

---

# E. Executive Justification

From a financial management perspective, the forward hedge provides the best balance between risk reduction and operational simplicity. It eliminates exchange-rate uncertainty without requiring the borrowing and investing transactions associated with the money-market hedge and avoids the premium expense required for an option strategy.

For a company seeking predictable cash flows, stable budgeting, and reduced earnings volatility, the forward hedge represents the most appropriate solution. While the company gives up the opportunity to benefit from favorable exchange-rate movements, it also eliminates the possibility of a significant decline in the U.S. dollar value of the receivable.

Based on the completed analysis and current market conditions, the forward hedge is the recommended strategy for managing this foreign exchange exposure.
